# FAQ (presentation prep)

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| What are the four task types? | 100 questions each, 400 in total, all from public datasets. **yes/no** (BoolQ): read a passage and answer yes or no. **classify** (AG News): label a news article as World, Sports, Business or Sci/Tech. **extract** (SQuAD): copy the answer to a question out of a passage; graded by an LLM judge. **math** (GSM8K): multi-step word problems; graded on the final number. |
| Why these four? | They differ a lot in difficulty and output length. yes/no and classify need one or two words, extract a short span, and math a worked solution, which is the longest output and the slowest and most energy-hungry to run locally. Small models come close to the cloud on yes/no and extract, and fall well behind on classify and math. |
| What is task-rule? | Route by task type only: yes/no and extract run locally, classify and math go to the cloud. The rule comes from the RQ1 results. Half the requests stay local, carbon is about half of always-cloud, accuracy is 94% of the cloud's, and it is the fastest policy. Its weakness: a request routed locally keeps the local answer whether it is right or not. |
| What is cascade? | The local model answers first. If its confidence (mean token log-probability of the answer) is above a per-task threshold, the local answer is kept; otherwise the request is sent to the cloud as well. Each threshold is the lowest one that keeps accuracy within 0.02 of always-cloud. An escalated request pays local plus cloud time and energy, so cascade is slow. |
| What is rule+cascade? | task-rule first: classify and math go straight to the cloud; yes/no and extract are answered locally, and the cloud is asked again when the local model is unsure. Accuracy reaches 98% of the cloud's, but about 73% of requests end up in the cloud, so carbon is higher than task-rule. |
| What is the oracle? | An upper bound: it knows in advance whether the local answer is right and only uses the cloud when it is wrong. No real router can do this. It keeps about 75% of requests local at cloud-level accuracy. The gap between cascade and the oracle shows that the confidence signal is the bottleneck. |
| What is fit-rule? | A per-model task rule: for each model, keep local the two tasks where it loses the least accuracy to the cloud. Gemma picks classify + extract and goes from 0.875 to 0.890; for Llama and Phi it picks the original rule. |
| What are confidence and AUROC? | Confidence is the mean log-probability of the tokens the model wrote; higher means more sure. AUROC measures how well confidence separates right from wrong answers: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. We get 0.52-0.83, so cascade often misjudges. |
| How is embodied carbon computed? | Embodied carbon is the carbon from manufacturing the hardware, spread over its lifetime. Low / mid / high assumptions; mid values here. **Laptop**: about 250 kg CO2e to make, used 4 years at 8 hours a day, so about 6 mg per active second, times the seconds the request takes. It is charged on both paths, because the laptop waits for the cloud answer too. **Cloud server**: an 8-GPU inference server at about 2,500 kg, 6 kW average, 5 years, 50% utilization, so about 19 g per kWh of server energy, times the energy of a cloud request (0.24 Wh). |
| What does embodied carbon change? | It adds about 42% to an always-local request (local operation uses so little energy that the laptop's share looks large) and about 9% to an always-cloud request. The ranking of the policies does not change. Network carbon is under 1%. These are estimates: Groq's LPU hardware has no published footprint, so a GPU server stands in, and training carbon is excluded. Details in `rq3/results/rq3_summary.md`, section 3. |
| What does the hourly grid change? | With the hour-by-hour MISO 2024 carbon intensity (mean 445 g/kWh, we had used a fixed 420), yearly carbon changes by under 1% for any policy that calls the cloud, and no routing decision on the GPU flips. |
| Why not route by carbon alone (carbon-aware)? | A local request emits far less than a cloud request, so a carbon-only router keeps everything local and accuracy drops to 0.75. Carbon works as a tie-breaker once quality is met, not as the main criterion. |
